## What S292 added

The `do {} while (0)` around the `lookup_animation_by_id` call is new and is worth keeping: the loop
note reweights the refs inside it, taking `cs` from `$s0` to the ROM's `$s1` and cutting the residual
from about 28 rows to **20**. `cs` is now correct; `id` and `target` remain swapped
(mine `id`->`$s0`, `target`->`$s2`; ROM `target`->`$s0`, `id`->`$s2`).

## Residual, now measured rather than argued (`tools/allocno_report.py`)

Baseline body, before the note:

| allocno | variable | refs | live | priority | reg | ROM wants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 74 | `cs` | 4 | 14 | 5714 | `$s0` | `$s1` |
| 72 | `id` | 3 | 13 | 2307 | `$s1` | `$s2` |
| 73 | `target` | 2 | 14 | 1428 | `$s2` | `$s0` |

The ROM's order is **target > cs > id**, the exact inverse of the ref-count order. `target` is a
pass-through parameter with two references (its entry home and the one call argument) and a live
range identical to `id`'s; `cs` has four references over the same window. For `target` to outrank
`cs` under `floor_log2(refs)*refs/live_length`, `cs`'s live length would have to exceed four times
`target`'s — impossible in a 39-instruction function where both are pinned to the same window.

Reweighting is the only knob that moves these, and it cannot separate `id` from `target`, because
their only weighted references are the two arguments of the *same* call:

| variant | id | cs | target | rows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| baseline | 2307 | 5714 | 1428 | ~28 |
| note round the call | 6153 | 5714 | 2142 | **20** |
| two nested notes | 7692 | 5714 | 5714 | 28 |
| `s32 a = id;` split inside the note | 6153 | 5714 | 2142 | 20 |

Every note that lifts `target` lifts `id` at least as much. Copy-based splits (`s32 t = target;`,
`s32 a = id;`) are propagated away by cse before flow counts references, so they change nothing —
the same coalescing that made the S288 bound-copy a no-op on `func_8005D334`.

## Why this is not simply the S213 verdict restated

S213 concluded "`cs` has forced max refs and minimum span, so it always colours `$s0` first". That
much is now disproved: the `do {} while (0)` note colours `cs` to `$s1` exactly as the ROM does, so
the class is steerable. What survives is narrower and better evidenced: **the ROM's `target` holds
the first callee-saved register while carrying two references, and no faithful C body gives a
two-reference pass-through parameter a higher `allocno_compare` priority than a four-reference local
over the same live range.**

Worth noting for the next attempt: the model "priority descending maps to ascending register number"
does **not** hold generally for call-crossing pseudos. The banked sibling `lookup_animation_by_id` in
the same file allocates `$v1`, `$a0`, `$v0`, `$a1`, `$s0` in descending priority order — register 2
lands third. So `find_reg`'s caller/callee-saved preference logic, not raw priority order, decides
which class a pseudo lands in, and the three-way rotation here may be reachable through that
mechanism rather than through priority arithmetic. That is the unexplored direction.

## Verdict

CARRY. Best body is above (20 rows, exact count and frame). Not a fresh smallest-first leaf. The next
slice should attack `find_reg`'s callee-saved preference path in `global.c` rather than
`allocno_compare` priorities, since the priority arithmetic is now measured and closed.
